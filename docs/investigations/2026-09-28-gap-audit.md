## 1. Rings of Power *Main Titles* misattributed to McCreary - Truth, High

`[[bear-mccreary]]` builds its entire "Notable Works" and "Compositional Signature" sections on *The Rings of Power – Main Titles*. `[[aba1-cinematic-form]]` uses the same piece as its worked example.

**Evidence:** `raw/You don't need better chords.pdf` (Composing Academy newsletter, 2026-03-20) says: *"Take The Rings of Power – Main Titles by Bear McCreary."* The wiki reproduced it faithfully, so this is an error in the source.

**World knowledge:** the *Main Title* theme of *The Rings of Power* (2022) was composed by **Howard Shore**. Bear McCreary composed the series score. The credit split was widely reported when the show launched. My confidence is high. The show's credits or soundtrack album credits would settle it.

**Why it matters:** an entity page describes a composer's signature using a piece he didn't write. The form analysis itself (A–B–A1–B1, timestamps, orchestration) is still valid as analysis; only the attribution is wrong.

**Action (for the wiki agent):**
- Re-attribute the example on `[[bear-mccreary]]` and `[[aba1-cinematic-form]]`.
- Either move the "signature" bullets to `[[howard-shore]]` or re-derive them from a real McCreary cue.
- Note on `[[you-dont-need-better-chords]]` that the source misattributes the cue.

## 2. Chromatic-mediant example states the wrong common tone - Truth, High

`[[chromatic-mediants-orchestration]]`, under "Chromatic Mediant, Defined", says: *"A minor–F minor share only the note A"*.

A minor is A–C–E and F minor is F–A♭–C, so the only shared tone is **C**. F minor contains A♭, not A.

**Evidence:** `raw/Write Dark Delicate or Mysical Music with Just Two Chords.pdf` says only that the chords *"share only a single common tone"* and gives the pair *"A minor - F minor"*. It never names the tone. **The error was introduced during synthesis.** The raw source is correct.

**Why it matters:** this is the definitional example on the page a learner reads first. The lint can't catch it: no other page contradicts it, and the lint doesn't re-derive music theory.

**Action:** change "the note A" to "the note C". The rest of the page checks out: minor-to-minor chromatic mediants are compound neo-Riemannian moves, not single basic transformations.

## 4. `[[god-chords]]` presents an uncited note as firm attributions - Truth, Medium

The page's only source is `raw/God chords.md`, a personal note (2026-03-17) with no citations. Its style ("If you want to create your own 'Hans Zimmer god chords,' try…") suggests it's pasted LLM output. The page hardens the note's hedging: the raw says *"some common techniques he employs"*, while the page says *"Zimmer's own version of the effect combines five specific, identifiable techniques"*. The page also lacks the "single third-party source" caveat that every entity page carries.

Specific problems:
- **"Shepard progression" (technique #3).** The page, following the raw, calls Dm–B♭–F–C–G–Dm a set of *"stepwise-ascending chords"*. From B♭ onward the roots rise by **fifths** (B♭→F→C→G→D), not by step, so the description contradicts its own example. Separately, Zimmer's well-documented Shepard-tone use is *Dunkirk* (2017), and the Batpod sound in *The Dark Knight*, not *Interstellar*. I'm fairly confident on *Dunkirk*: Zimmer and Nolan discussed it publicly. I'm unsure whether *Interstellar* uses the technique at all.
- **Unverified specifics.** These claims are specific enough to look authoritative, but nothing in the raw note supports them: that "Stay" and "Cornfield Chase" use I–V–vi–IV, that "Mountains" is built on Cmaj9(♯11), and that "Rise" uses a plagal IV–I. I can't confirm or refute them from memory. A transcription or score reduction would settle them.
- The lint's §4 checked this page only against other wiki pages. That consistency pass made the page look vetted when its evidence is weak.

**Action:**
- Add the standard single-uncited-source caveat.
- Soften "Zimmer's own version… combines" back to the raw's hedging.
- Fix "stepwise" to "a chain of rising fifths".
- Change the *Interstellar* Shepard-tone attribution to *Dunkirk*, or mark it unverified.

## 5. Synthwave beat placement - Truth, Medium

`[[synthwave-production]]` says: *"Kick and snare emphasize beats 1 and 3."* Copied faithfully from `raw/Synthwave production.md` line 11 (*"Emphasize beats 1 and 3 with kick and snare"*).

The genre's standard drum-machine pattern, inherited from 80s pop, is kick on 1 and 3 (or four-on-the-floor) with the snare as a backbeat on **2 and 4**. Snare on 1 and 3 would sound wrong for the style. The note most likely compressed "kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4" into one line. My confidence is high.

**Action:** correct the sentence, and add a note that the raw text is ambiguous.
